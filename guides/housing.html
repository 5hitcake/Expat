Renting without a local guarantor

Most listings ask for a "garantía propietaria" — someone who owns property in Buenos Aires and vouches for you. As a newcomer, you won't have that. Here's what people actually do instead.

Rental rules here have changed more than once recently (the old rental law was repealed by DNU 70/2023, and contracts now run on the Civil and Commercial Code by default) — last checked August 2026. Terms are more negotiable than they used to be, which is good news, but also means "the rules" vary more by landlord/agency than they used to.

Phase 2 · A standard rental contract, no local guarantor

0/5

The most common substitute for a garantía propietaria is a seguro de caución — an insurance policy that pays the landlord if you don't, which you're on the hook to repay. It's what most foreigners end up using.
