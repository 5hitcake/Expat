Which neighborhood actually fits you

Not a bureaucratic process this time — just an honest comparison of where people actually end up, and why.

Peso prices below are a rough snapshot, not a quote — last checked August 2026. Rents move with inflation faster than any guide can track; treat the comparisons between neighborhoods as the useful part, and check live listings (Zonaprop, Argenprop) for current numbers.

Palermo (Soho, Hollywood, Chico)

The default first choice: best cafés, nightlife, and coworking density, plus the most furnished short-term rentals in the city. Soho leans party/tourist, Hollywood more residential, Chico quieter and pricier. Also the most expensive tier. Best if you want convenience and social life over saving money.

Villa Crespo

Roughly 20–25% cheaper than Palermo for a similar lifestyle — a 10-minute walk to Soho, its own growing restaurant scene, subte line B. Best if you want the Palermo experience without the Palermo price tag.

Belgrano

The family neighborhood: the most bilingual/private schools in the city, three subte lines, green space at the Barrancas, and dining that rivals Palermo. Also one of the safer areas. Best if you're moving with kids or want a quieter, well-connected base.

Recoleta

Central, elegant, close to major universities and museums. Quieter and more traditional than Palermo. Best if you want a classic, walkable, culture-heavy base — popular with students for that reason.

Núñez / Las Cañitas / Palermo Chico

Consistently rank among the safest parts of the city — more residential and upscale, quieter than central Palermo. Best if safety and calm matter more to you than having nightlife on your doorstep.

Caballito

The best value-for-money among central options: subte line A, a genuine neighborhood feel, popular with families and students, noticeably cheaper than Palermo or Belgrano. Best if you want to be central without paying Palermo prices.
