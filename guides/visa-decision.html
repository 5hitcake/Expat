Which visa actually fits you

This decides everything downstream — whether you even qualify for a DNI. Picking the convenient-sounding option isn't always the one that gets you there.

Income thresholds and processing details change — last checked August 2026. This is a starting point for narrowing your options, not a substitute for checking current requirements or talking to an immigration lawyer for anything borderline.

Quick sort

Start here — it's less about nationality or budget than about where your money comes from.

Rentista

Needs steady passive income from abroad — roughly USD 2,000/month in practice (the official minimum is lower, but applications near it get scrutinized more). Only passive income counts: dividends, interest, royalties, rent, pensions. Salary and freelance fees don't qualify. Granted for 1 year, renewable up to 3, and leads to permanent residency after 2 consecutive years. This is a Residencia Temporaria — it qualifies you for a DNI.

Trabajador Migrante

For dependent employment — you need a signed pre-contract (tasks, hours, duration, pay, matching the sector's labor agreement) certified by a notary or Migraciones, plus your employer's AFIP registration. Granted for up to 3 years, renewable while the job continues. Also a Residencia Temporaria — qualifies you for a DNI. Your employer typically drives most of this paperwork.

Digital Nomad Visa

Built for freelancers or remote employees paid from abroad — around USD 2,500/month, mostly online application, faster and simpler than the other two. But: it's a multiple-entry visa valid roughly 6 months (renewable to about a year), and it isn't clearly the same Residencia Temporaria category that unlocks a DNI. If getting a DNI eventually matters to you, confirm this directly before counting on it — you may need to transition to Rentista or Trabajador Migrante later.
